whenever a company decides to go public its first task is to select underwriters , they act as financial midwives to a new issue , usually play a triple role: first provide company with procedural and financial advice , then buy issue and finaly resell it to public. established ones are careful of there reputation and wont handle a new issue unless they belive facts have been presented fairly , thus in addition to handling sale of company ' s issue they in effect give there seal of approval to it. they prepare a registration statement for approval of SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission) and need to check that issue complies with so-called blue-sky laws of each state that regulate sales of securities within state. while it is awaiting approval they begin to firm up issue price and arange a road show to talk to potential investors , immediately after clearance from SEC they fix price and after that enter into a firm commitment to buy stock and offer it to public , when they haven ' t still found any reason not to do it
